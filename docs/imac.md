# In-Mission Air Commander (IMAC)

IMAC provides optional in-mission scripted air coordination, without an LLM. Enable it under **Mission Generator → In-Mission Air Commander** and regenerate the mission. BLUE and RED have independent commanders and enable switches. It is off by default.

## Authority and intelligence

Each side needs a live, airborne AWACS with an operating radar, or a live command centre with working power. Command centres do not create contacts: IMAC fuses observations from that side's aircraft and ground/naval sensors. It assumes coalition-wide radio sharing; it does not simulate regional radio coverage between aircraft and command centres. Losing authority releases temporary orders rather than replacing the original missions.

A standalone command centre can provide authority without IADS membership or a radio-antenna link. This does not provide detection: an aircraft, ground radar or naval sensor still needs to observe the hostile aircraft. Explicit power dependencies and definite IADS power failures are respected.

Air tracks come from unit-controller visual, optical, radar and IRST detections and require known range. DCS reports four flags for a currently visible contact, without memory vectors; only after that positional detection is confirmed does IMAC sample its current position and velocity. For a contact no longer visible, IMAC uses only the sensor's last-known position, velocity and timestamp. Observations expire after 30 seconds. It does not sample undetected aircraft, infer their mission from names, or use their exported flight plans to classify them. Bombers, fighters and other broad classes are used only when the detector knows the type. Identified cruise and anti-ship missiles can also trigger interception; other weapons and ground contacts are excluded. Weapon contacts do not provide launcher intelligence. An interceptor's own reports are ignored while assigned, because DCS's `AttackGroup` task itself grants detection of its target.

SAM avoidance uses campaign-known sites, known naval air defences and currently shared Realistic CAS discoveries. Hidden ground units are not sampled before discovery. Ground threat heights are measured above the launcher's MSL elevation, using the same verified ceiling data as AFAC navigation. Unknown ceilings remain unsafe at every height. Batteries shown dark (grey) on the campaign map are excluded for that generated turn, without disabling their independent point defences. Destroyed launchers, missing compatible tracking radars and definite runtime IADS shutdowns also remove threats; ordinary radar silence and autonomous operation alone do not. AFAC navigation uses the same exclusions. Verified mobile transit defences count only while the transit script reports them deployed.

Inactive late-activation units are excluded from flight control, command authority, sensor reports, new air tracks and active SAM envelopes. DCS can report these units as existing and airborne before activation; existence alone is not treated as participation in the mission.

## Decisions

| Flight | Temporary response | Return condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BARCAP / TARCAP on station | Nearest available patrol intercepts an approaching bomber/attack aircraft or a threat to a vulnerable friendly flight, including outside the native 50-NM radius | Contact expiry, withdrawal, fuel/ammunition/damage limits, unsafe pursuit, original station end or loss of command |
| Fighter-capable aircraft returning after SPLIT / RACETRACK END | Can reinforce the same interception pool, including against identified cruise and anti-ship missiles | Same safety limits, followed by resumption of the original return route; no new orders during landing approach |
| AWACS, tankers, transports, ferry, attack and DEAD flights | Move to a safe orbit away from closing fighters or nearby unclassified contacts | At least 30 seconds clear of the danger radius plus 10 NM, including the first three minutes of the return leg, with sufficient fuel and time remaining |
| Attack flight before ingress | Hold in a safe area if a known SAM blocks the immediate route and a DEAD/SEAD flight is working nearby or in the same package | Resume when the blocked corridor clears; otherwise recover at BINGO or when all supporting DEAD/SEAD flights are destroyed, cancelled or complete |

One interceptor is assigned per detected hostile group or missile. Assignment considers distance, fuel for the detour and return, air-to-air ammunition, damage, the original station deadline, predicted target movement and terrain/SAM corridors. Maximum intercept range defaults to 120 NM, distance from the patrol anchor to 80 NM, and withdrawal pursuit to 180 seconds. Returning fighters use their position at assignment as the fixed diversion anchor and budget fuel to their original destination. Every intercept also has a 15-minute hard deadline. These are constraints, not a promise to engage every contact inside the outer range.

Returning eligibility uses the aircraft's configured CAP/intercept capabilities. F-16, F/A-18 and F-15EX strike or escort flights can help after their return waypoint. Planned refuelling, BINGO and landing approach block assignments. Active patrols retain their original station deadlines. A patrol in a SAM avoidance orbit can engage aircraft inside its local orbit zone and take a separate IMAC intercept through a clear corridor. When that intercept ends, it returns to the orbit. Missile interception uses a temporary native route with cruise/anti-ship engagement filters at the fighter's current altitude.

Evasion samples several directions, rejects corridors that cross known SAM rings, checks room for the turn and favours increasing separation from all nearby threats. The commander does not move ground units, attack ground targets itself, or order emergency scrambles.

Evasion does not end merely because escaping has stopped the closure. IMAC checks the planned return leg and observed hostile motion before resuming. Its temporary evasion lease can renew while danger persists; native BINGO, station deadlines and loss of command still release it.

An attack holding for DEAD/SEAD can also evade approaching fighters, then resume waiting. Temporary evasion by the supporting flight does not cancel the attack, even if it moves farther away. Waiting has no fixed time limit: it ends at BINGO, when the corridor clears, or when all assigned support flights are destroyed, cancelled or complete. If support ends but the corridor remains unsafe, the attack returns to base. An airborne human DEAD/SEAD flight can provide support without receiving IMAC orders.

## Route and task ownership

Interception and orbit orders are native `ControlledTask` leases pushed above the existing mission. After a hold or evasion before ingress, attack flights with locked arrival times resume at the next waypoint using planned speeds and their remaining native tasks. Ending a lease does not call `popTask`, restart the flight at waypoint one, extend a patrol's station time or repeat completed attack waypoints. Native BINGO behaviour remains enabled. IMAC yields to planned refuelling and the reactive BARCAP refuelling controller. Player/Client groups, including mixed groups, never receive route hooks or orders, but can be protected by AI patrols. Runtime player takeover also releases the lease.

Cancellation uses the final generated recovery waypoints, retaining carrier links and off-map/despawn actions. The recovery search checks known threats and terrain and can add a bounded detour. If it cannot find a corridor, it keeps an existing safe hold, reports the problem and retries; it never substitutes an unchecked direct route. BINGO, player control and loss of command still take precedence. This is not a full terrain-following pathfinder.

Active AFAC and air-assault navigation remain under their existing controllers. Escort and Fighter Sweep flights are exempt from protective fighter evasion by default. Once they reach Join or Sweep Start, IMAC can give them a temporary intercept against the detected fighter threatening an evading member of their package. The escort leash releases its ROE control once the package's native split flag ends the escort task. Fighter-capable aircraft can then be reassigned once they reach their own return waypoint. For manual F10 orders, use **F10 → Other → IMAC → Pause / release flights for manual control** first. Coalition-wide resume and status controls, plus per-flight pause/resume controls, are available. Pausing releases only IMAC's leases.

## Settings and validation

Both commanders and coalition order reports are enabled when IMAC is enabled. The default decision interval is 10 seconds. Intercept range, patrol leash, pursuit time and danger radius are configurable. Holding and evasion use renewable task leases, not mission-cancellation deadlines. AWACS and long-range radars are sampled every cycle; up to 64 other sensors per side rotate fairly. A source processes at most 512 detection entries per pass. Short-lived local-only contacts may be missed between scans. The event history is capped at 128 entries. Native-query errors are rate-limited in the log, and a decision-cycle failure stops automatic command and releases its leases.

**Divert attacks and BARCAPs away from SAM threat rings** is on by default and also applies to TARCAP and returning-fighter interception corridors. **Never divert DEAD/SEAD missions for SAM rings** is on by default and covers DEAD, SEAD, SEAD Escort and SEAD Sweep. Turn it off to allow SAM avoidance for those missions. When SAM avoidance is off, its exemption is checked and disabled. Support-aircraft evasion still chooses SAM-safe routes; terrain and fuel limits remain active.

**Divert flights away from enemy fighters** is a separate, default-on protective-evasion switch. **Never divert Escort or Fighter Sweep away from fighters** is also on by default. When fighter evasion is off, that exemption is checked and disabled; when it is on, the exemption is editable. These settings do not disable native combat tasks or IMAC CAP interception, and the dependencies are enforced during mission generation as well as in the settings dialog.

**Enable BLUE commander** and **Enable RED commander** are independent campaign settings. **Debug messages and decision logging** is off by default. When enabled, the owning coalition sees orders and rejection reasons, limited to four debug messages per minute. Detailed `IMAC|DEBUG|` log entries also include command authority, active-order fuel, waypoint and remaining lease time. Repeated messages and active-order snapshots are throttled to one per minute per flight/topic. Debug is independent of normal order reports. F10 commander status includes the current source of command authority.

**Show OPFOR debug messages on BLUE** is an optional testing aid, off by default. With debug and the RED commander enabled, it mirrors RED decisions, rejection reasons and active-order details to BLUE with a separate eight-message-per-minute limit. BLUE command does not need to be enabled. Routine authority and availability messages stay in the log. Normal order reports remain coalition-only.

Tests execute the production scripts in Lua 5.1 and verify detection, authority, fuel limits, task ownership, geometry, recovery and `.miz` generation. Missile detection/task contracts and returning-fighter eligibility are covered by automated tests; actual missile engagement and returning-fighter diversions still need native DCS observation. Earlier native DCS tests have verified both-side CAP redirection beyond 50 NM, bounded pursuit and return, known-SAM interception limits, vulnerable-AWACS evasion and return, attack holding and resumption, standalone command-centre authority, independent coalition switches, inactive-unit exclusion, F10 controls, native refuelling and carrier landing. Campaign-scale interactions still need observation. IMAC does not change DCS's native engagement or reaction options; its safety buffers are conservative estimates, not a guarantee that native AI will never enter a threat envelope. Logs use the `IMAC|` prefix.
